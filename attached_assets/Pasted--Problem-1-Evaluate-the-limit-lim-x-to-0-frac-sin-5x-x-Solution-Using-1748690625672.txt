### Problem 1: Evaluate the limit
\[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{x} \]

**Solution:**

Using the standard limit \(\lim_{u \to 0} \frac{\sin(u)}{u} = 1\), we can rewrite the expression:

\[
\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{5x} \cdot 5 = 5 \cdot \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{5x} = 5 \cdot 1 = 5
\]

**Final Answer:** 5

### Problem 3: Compute the line integral of the vector field \( \mathbf{F}(x, y) = (x^2 + y)\mathbf{i} + (x - y^2)\mathbf{j} \) along the curve \( C \), which is the unit circle oriented counterclockwise.

**Solution:**

The unit circle can be parameterized by \( \mathbf{r}(t) = (\cos t, \sin t) \) for \( t \in [0, 2\pi] \).

The line integral is given by:
\[ \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_0^{2\pi} \mathbf{F}(\cos t, \sin t) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) \, dt \]

Calculate \(\mathbf{r}'(t) = (-\sin t, \cos t)\).

Substitute into \(\mathbf{F}\):
\[ \mathbf{F}(\cos t, \sin t) = (\cos^2 t + \sin t, \cos t - \sin^2 t) \]

Calculate the dot product:
\[ \mathbf{F}(\cos t, \sin t) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) = (\cos^2 t + \sin t)(-\sin t) + (\cos t - \sin^2 t)\cos t \]

Simplify:
\[ = -\cos^2 t \sin t - \sin^2 t + \cos^2 t \cos t - \sin^2 t \cos t \]

Combine terms:
\[ = -\sin t(\cos^2 t + \sin t) + \cos t(\cos^2 t - \sin^2 t) \]

Using \(\cos^2 t - \sin^2 t = \cos 2t\), the integral simplifies to:
\[ \int_0^{2\pi} -\sin t \cos^2 t - \sin^3 t + \cos t \cos 2t \, dt \]

This integral evaluates to zero due to symmetry and periodicity over the interval \([0, 2\pi]\).

**Final Answer:** 0

### Problem 4: Determine whether the series \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3n+1}{2n^2+n}\) converges or diverges.

**Solution:**

Consider the series \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3n+1}{2n^2+n}\).

As \(n \to \infty\), the dominant terms in the numerator and denominator are \(3n\) and \(2n^2\), respectively. Thus, the series behaves like \(\frac{3n}{2n^2} = \frac{3}{2n}\).

The series \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n}\) is the harmonic series, which diverges.

Using the Limit Comparison Test with \(b_n = \frac{1}{n}\):
\[ \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{\frac{3n+1}{2n^2+n}}{\frac{1}{n}} = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{3n^2 + n}{2n^2 + n} = \frac{3}{2} \]

Since \(\frac{3}{2}\) is a positive finite number, the series \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3n+1}{2n^2+n}\) diverges by the Limit Comparison Test.

**Final Answer:** Diverges

### Problem 5: Use Newton's Method with an initial approximation of \(x_0 = 1\) to find a root of \(f(x) = x^3 - 2x + 1\). Perform three iterations.

**Solution:**

Newton's Method formula:
\[ x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)} \]

First, find \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2\).

**Iteration 1:**
- \(x_0 = 1\)
- \(f(x_0) = 1^3 - 2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 0\)
- \(f'(x_0) = 3 \cdot 1^2 - 2 = 1\)

\[ x_1 = 1 - \frac{0}{1} = 1 \]

**Iteration 2:**
- \(x_1 = 1\)
- \(f(x_1) = 0\)
- \(f'(x_1) = 1\)

\[ x_2 = 1 - \frac{0}{1} = 1 \]

**Iteration 3:**
- \(x_2 = 1\)
- \(f(x_2) = 0\)
- \(f'(x_2) = 1\)

\[ x_3 = 1 - \frac{0}{1} = 1 \]

Since \(f(x_0) = 0\), \(x_0 = 1\) is already a root, and further iterations do not change the result.

**Final Answer:** The root is \(x = 1\)
